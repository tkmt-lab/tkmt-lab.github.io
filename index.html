How the work is checked

Each release keeps different kinds of evidence apart, and says which applies to each claim:

Formal proofChecked by the Lean 4 kernel. Separately, the Comparator tool checks that the proof proves exactly a statement written in advance by a different agent. Anyone can re-run the build; each repository's GitHub Actions workflow rebuilds the main modules and checks their axioms on every push. Any step from the paper to the formal statement that is not in Lean is said so in the repository.
Independent derivationA second proof written by a separate team that did not see the first proof or its code.
AI reviewProse read and recalculated by an AI reviewer. Not human peer review.
Finite computationExhaustive or sampled checks, with their exact ranges.

What is not yet established — for example human peer review or novelty — is stated in each repository. Corrections are listed openly.

How this research is done

The research is carried out with AI systems (OpenAI Codex and Anthropic Claude Code) working as separate teams for exploration, independent verification, literature search and formalization. I direct the work, make the decisions, and am responsible for what is released, its provenance and the stated limits of verification. AI systems are not listed as authors.

Built on the work of others

  • A. Bille, V. Buchstaber and E. Spodarev, who defined generalized Stone–Wales paths and collected open problems on fullerenes. Our question comes from their work.
  • M. Goldberg and H. S. M. Coxeter, after whom the Goldberg–Coxeter construction is named, and M. Dutour Sikirić and M. Deza, who developed it for plane graphs together with the theory of zigzags.
  • J. E. Graver, whose catalogue of highly symmetric fullerenes gives the families we study, and K. Kutnar and D. Marušič, whose work on cyclic edge-connectivity of fullerenes we rely on.
  • J. R. Büchi, C. C. Elgot and B. A. Trakhtenbrot, whose theorem connecting logic on words with finite automata is the last step of the periodicity proof.
  • The developers of Lean 4 and mathlib, and of the Comparator tool and the independent proof kernels used to check our proofs.

I am grateful to them, and I hope this work adds something new to the field they opened. The papers cite these and other sources in full.
